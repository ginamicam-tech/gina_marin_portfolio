    ## About the work

    *IN_Huellas (IN_Footprints)* opens this sequence of illustrative investigations with a series of studies centred on the feet, understood as the body’s point of anchorage in the material world. Initially conceived as a photoperformance, the work used serial photographic captures to create a rhythmic sequence, approaching chronophotography or a movement study. These records were subsequently translated into drawings on paper.

    The work is driven by an existential question: what does it mean to take a step in the material world when digital environments seem to offer a light, almost disembodied experience? In this sense, it enters into dialogue with Byung-Chul Han’s reflections (2012) on the erosion of worldly resistance in digital society: a frictionless hyperreality in which bodily presence loses its density.

    ## From photoperformance to drawing

    Developed within the Visual Narratives Laboratory, the series originated in a staged photoperformance. I first constructed makeshift brick shoes, fastening bricks to my feet with ropes. The action unfolded through movement and the gradual untying of the ropes, ending with the feet released from their bindings. Serial photographs documented this progression from constraint to release.

    I translated these photographic records into drawings on paper, then developed the illustrated sequences into digital variations. Using transparency, graphic layers and compositional rearrangements, I explored how a physical gesture becomes a visual trace.

    **IN_Huellas, 2019.** Mixed media: photoperformance documentation, illustration and digital editing.

    ## Between lightness and weight

    My intention was to register how the body seeks to reassert its earthly presence, even as it risks becoming weightless in virtual space. A paradox emerges: while the foot dematerialises into a visual trajectory in digital environments, its physical footprint can feel heavy, carrying an almost inexplicable exhaustion, as though each step condensed the friction absent from virtual experience.

    The performative action therefore moved between gestures of advance and retreat. Some sequences show bare feet moving freely; others show feet burdened by bricks tied with ropes. These contrasting actions give material form to the tension between lightness and weight, freedom of movement and the resistance of the ground.
