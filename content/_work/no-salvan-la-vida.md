## Context & Challenge

They Do Not Save Life, Nor Sustain the Soul brought together the practices of Paz Herrera, Nayra Vallejos, Florencia Aravena and Gina Marin in Córdoba in 2019. Presented by Espacio Garín at Centro Cultural Municipal Casa de Pepino, the exhibition opened on 16 August and continued until 8 September, with live music by Gluko Lin at the opening. Its title questioned heroic claims for art while creating a place for people to gather and look together. The collective challenge was to connect distinct artistic languages within a shared spatial proposition without erasing the differences that gave each practice its own voice.

## Approach & Contribution

My participation in Atmósfera Collective combined installation practice with project coordination, shared art direction and communication. I helped connect the exhibition concept, preparation and public presentation, working with the other artists rather than treating their contributions as interchangeable parts. The documented process brought artistic decisions, production requirements and public communication into one shared direction. Photographs of the installation and opening provide evidence of the resulting encounter, while the moving-image record preserves spatial details and the presence of visitors. This case focuses on the documented 2019 exhibition, showing how a collective project made room for individual work and a public experience within the same setting.

Process and spatial logic

## From proposition to public experience.
{: #exhibition-process-title}

01 

### A collective frame

The title established a common tension while leaving room for four distinct practices and visual positions.

02 

### Works in relation

The exhibition was shaped through proximity: each contribution held its own voice while becoming part of a larger visual conversation.

03 

### Public identity

The announcement carried the exhibition’s bodily, layered language into the public realm and located the project within a local cultural network.

04 

### Shared encounter

Visual work, music and the opening programme created a social situation around the exhibition rather than a purely contemplative display.
