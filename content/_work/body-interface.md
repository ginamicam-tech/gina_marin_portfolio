    ## The thesis

    *IN/Pressions of a Body. Interface: Poetics, Clinical Practice, and Its Symbolic Activation in the Age of Liquid Architecture.*

    My research asks how we can remain present in our bodies while so much of everyday life takes place through screens. I approach the body as an interface: a place where sensation, images, memories and digital systems meet. “Liquid architecture” describes the changing digital spaces we inhabit, and the ways our presence moves between physical places and online environments.

    Through photography, performance, drawing and printmaking, I explore the pressures of constant visibility and the possibility of choosing how, when and how much of ourselves we share. The work treats bodily attention, movement and image-making as ways to understand these pressures. Here, clinical practice is an artistic approach to observation and care, rather than medical diagnosis or treatment.

    ## How can art bring us back to the body?

    Two photoperformances form the centre of the thesis. [Clinical Fold](/work/clinical-fold/) uses a sewn plastic costume, butoh movement and the confined space of a bathroom to explore restriction and bodily presence. [Self Archive](/work/self-archive/) follows my move to Copenhagen through repeated self-portraits, layered with the architecture of everyday journeys.

    One works with enclosure; the other with movement through a new city. Both ask how an image can carry lived experience rather than simply display a body. Earlier drawings, prints and artist’s books trace how this enquiry developed.

    ## A practice of attention

    My method moves between three connected actions: noticing an impression or a pressure, returning to bodily awareness through breath and movement, and giving that experience a form through performance, images or writing. These actions feed back into one another. Editing is part of the research: repetition, white lines, brightness and transparency change how a body appears and how an image is read.
    {: #method-title}

    ## A living archive

    On Are.na, photographs, drawings and texts form an archive that can be rearranged and connected to other people’s collections. I use it as a space for slower looking and shared thinking, where an artwork can acquire new relationships over time.

    ## What carries into my practice

    The research brings attention, bodily agency and care into the way I make and share work. It provides a foundation for experiments with performance, materials and participatory environments, and for thinking about technology through the experience of the people using it.
