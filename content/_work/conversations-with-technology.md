    ## Drawing, mediation, and experimental publishing

    This body of work brings drawing, poetic writing, and experimental publishing into dialogue with questions developed through my thesis research on technology and subjectivity. It explores how mediated experience shapes our perception of time, our sense of proximity, and the ways we recognise ourselves. Technology enters the work as a condition of everyday experience: something that participates in how we feel, remember, communicate, and imagine.
- layout: newspaper
  text: |-
    ## Drawing as inquiry

    Drawing becomes a way of thinking through these relationships. Accumulated lines, dense black marks, interrupted contours, and red-orange fields create a visual language suspended between bodily sensation and abstract terrain. Forms appear to gather, dissolve, and reassemble. Their uncertain boundaries evoke a subject whose identity takes shape through encounters with images, signals, other bodies, and technical environments.

    The relationship between drawing and technology unfolds as a conversation. The drawn mark carries the duration and pressure of a gesture; its repetition across printed copies brings that gesture into a different rhythm of circulation. This movement raises a central question within the research: what happens to an intimate trace when it becomes reproducible, shareable, and available to others? The publications hold together these different scales of experience—the singular act of making a mark and the collective life an image acquires through distribution.
- layout: newspaper
  text: |-
    ## Writing through mediation

    The accompanying texts extend this inquiry through fragmented dialogue, unanswered questions, and shifting voices. References to radio frequencies, artificiality, mechanical movement, distance, and reflection place emotional experience within a field of transmission. Closeness emerges as something negotiated through mediation. Loss persists as residue; identity appears as an ongoing process of becoming. The writing gives these tensions a temporal structure, allowing hesitation, interruption, and return to shape the act of reading.
- layout: newspaper
  text: |-
    ## The publication as a research space

    Within this framework, the fanzine functions as a material space for research. The page brings language and image into contact while allowing their differences to remain visible. Changes in density, empty space, and the relationship between text and drawing invite the reader to move between looking and reading, recognition and uncertainty. Handling the publication introduces another dimension: thought becomes something that can be held, revisited, and passed between people.
- layout: newspaper
  text: |-
    ## Circulation and encounter

    Presented at Copperweld, a fair dedicated to experimental editions, the work entered a context of independent publishing and direct exchange. This setting is integral to its conceptual significance. Circulation becomes part of the inquiry into connection: a publication travels from one person to another, creating an encounter through a material object.

    Presented at **Copperweld, Experimental Editions Fair**, Centro Cultural España Córdoba, Argentina, **30 November 2021**.
- layout: newspaper
  text: |-
    ## In dialogue with the thesis

    Read in relation to my thesis, these works articulate an early investigation into technologically mediated subjectivity. They approach drawing as a method of inquiry and publishing as a way of making that inquiry public. Their central concern is how to sustain sensitivity, ambiguity, and the possibility of connection within the systems through which contemporary experience takes form.
