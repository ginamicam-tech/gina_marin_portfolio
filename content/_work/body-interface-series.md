These drawings explore the body as an interface: a meeting place for physical sensation, memory and the flows of digital life. Figures emerge from dense networks of marks, sometimes fragmenting or merging with their surroundings. The body is not presented as a fixed outline, but as a presence that changes through its relationship with the space around it.

In her thesis, Gina describes drawing as a mental and bodily flow, guided by intuitive, calligraphic gestures. Black ink, occasional red accents and the white of the paper create organic networks that evoke the movement of data, thoughts and impulses. Faces and bodily fragments become part of the same texture as the space they inhabit. This approach connects the series to her investigation of “liquid architecture”: the shifting spaces of digital experience, and the search for a felt, embodied presence within them.
