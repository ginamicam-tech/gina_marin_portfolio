## Context & Challenge

Altar Women and LGBTI+ Lives in Struggle connects collaborative research, graphic ceramics and digital access in a living archive. Initiated by Silvia Barrios in 2018, the project developed through international open calls and collective research within MediaLab #ComunidadCulturaSolidaria. Its 2020 edition at the Cultural Kirchner Center in Buenos Aires was curated by Kekena Corvalán. Portraits and QR-linked biographies placed histories of struggle into a shared exhibition space, connecting a physical encounter with further reading. The central challenge was both artistic and public: how could a distributed body of research become accessible while retaining the specificity of the lives represented within it?

## Approach & Contribution

I participated in the artist-researcher collective, with a contribution documented in this portfolio through project coordination, art direction and communication. My work connected visual and audiovisual communication with the exhibition’s shared concept, alongside content, digital access and documentation developed with collaborators. The distinction between my contribution and the project’s overall authorship matters: Silvia Barrios’s artistic direction and Kekena Corvalán’s curatorship remain credited below. Installation photographs, graphic materials and the virtual exhibition record show different forms of access to the archive. They document a collective cultural production and its public presentation, without assigning the whole project or its research to a single author.

Process and spatial logic

## From proposition to public experience.
{: #exhibition-process-title}

01 

### Collective research

Lives were selected and contextualised through a distributed research process, making each biography part of a wider political and social map.

02 

### A shared image language

Distinct portraits were translated into a direct black and white graphic register so individual stories could remain visible inside one coherent system.

03 

### Material and digital access

Printed surfaces and QR codes connected physical presence to extended biographies, allowing the installation to continue beyond the room.

04 

### Spatial activation

Suspended images, plants, conversation and projection formed a porous environment that visitors could enter rather than observe from a distance.
